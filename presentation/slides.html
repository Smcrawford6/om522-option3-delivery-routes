The decision

Sadie Crawford

Who decides, and what

Every morning a dispatcher at one depot assigns each customer to a vehicle and chooses the order of stops on each route, before any driver leaves.

What makes it hard

  • Capacity: a route's total demand cannot exceed the vehicle's capacity.
  • Time windows: service must start inside the window. Arriving early means waiting; arriving late is not allowed.
  • Shifts: leave at shift start and return by shift end.
  • Every customer is served exactly once.

How we score a plan

  • Primary: total travel time (excludes waiting and service).
  • Secondary: vehicles used.
  • Plus computation time.

Our added assumption

A route may not cross itself on the map. A driver shouldn't cut back across their own path. Different routes may cross. Here travel time equals straight-line distance (within 0.0005 min), so uncrossing a route also shortens it.

Method 1: the rule to beat

Sadie Crawford

Nearest feasible neighbor, one vehicle at a time

  1. Take vehicles in identifier order; each leaves at its shift start.
  2. Drive to the nearest unserved customer (ties by ID) that
    · fits the remaining capacity,
    · can start service by its window end,
    · still allows a return by the shift end.
  3. No customer qualifies → return; the next vehicle starts.
  4. Anyone left unserved → “no feasible decision found”.

Deterministic, no seed. Implemented as written, so it doesn't apply our crossing rule: 7 of its routes cross themselves on the large case.

Method 2: the researched method

Zacc Gleason
1

Construct

Solomon (1987) I1 insertion. Seed a route, then insert the customer with the best score: a bonus for being far from the depot minus its insertion cost (the added travel blended with how far it pushes later stops back). Every window is checked. 12 parameter starts.

2

Improve

Local search with the brief's moves: relocate 1–3 customers (or-opt), exchange between routes, reverse a segment (2-opt), and swap route tails (2-opt*). Each customer is paired with its 12 nearest neighbors.

3

Ruin & recreate

Large neighborhood search (Shaw 1998; Ropke & Pisinger 2006): remove 4 to 30 customers, reinsert them (regret or greedy), improve, and accept by simulated annealing. 4 seeded chains; keep the best.

Representation: one ordered list of customers per vehicle. Each route caches its load, its service starts, and the latest feasible start at every stop.

Stopping rule: 4 chains × 2,000 iterations (a fixed count); the 300 s limit is only a safety cap. Seed 522; chain c uses Random(100·seed + c), so reruns are identical (tested).

Recommendation

Annabelle Kemp

Recommend

Adopt the researched method

Solomon I1 + local search + ruin and recreate, with the no-self-crossing rule. Evidence: one 45-customer development day (the held-out case has 60).

Expected benefit

612 fewer driving minutes a day

36% less planned driving (1,683.4 → 1,071.2 min) with 3 fewer vehicles (11 → 8); about 593 fewer minutes on an average uncertain day.

Tradeoff accepted

About 40 s of computing instead of milliseconds. Tighter routes, so slightly more days with at least one late stop (7 vs 6 of 30; 12 vs 8 at +10% congestion).

Would change if…

a day is judged by whether any window is missed and traffic runs ≥ 10% above plan: then the rule has fewer bad days (8 vs 12 of 30; 23 vs 28 at +30%), at about 652 more driving minutes a day. If zero missed windows is required, neither fixed plan qualifies; plan with buffered travel times or replan during the day.

Limitations, and thank you

Zacc Gleason

Limitations

  • A heuristic with no proof of optimality. The four chains on the large case end between 2,155.9 and 2,175.0 min; on medium our default seed stops at 1,071.159, and we know a plan at 1,070.565.
  • It plans for exact travel times with no slack, which is why heavier congestion is what would change the recommendation.
  • It minimizes travel only; fewer vehicles is a side effect, not a target.
  • The evidence is four synthetic instances from one generator, plus one infeasible edge case.
  • No-crossing is our rule. If no crossing-free plan fits the fleet, the tool relaxes it and says so in summary.md.
